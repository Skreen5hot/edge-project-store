## The direction

> The contract enforces project structure and document management, not pipeline process rules. SDD will take a
> "normalish" schema as its input, whatever produced it.

This reverses one requirement from the first review, "normalized schema as the structural source of truth", which
v0.1 implemented as **pipeline readiness** (R-E7). This proposal removes it and nothing else.

## What changes

**Removed:**

- the third column of §3, "Structural root required for pipeline readiness";
- **R-E7** in full. Its identifier is retired, not reused ("minted, never renumbered");
- from each artifact in the report: `pipelineReady` and `notReadyBecause`, with its four codes `OUT-OF-DATE`,
  `NOT-CURRENT`, `NO-STRUCTURAL-ROOT` and `STRUCTURAL-ROOT-MISMATCH`;
- the two context terms `pipelineReady` and `notReadyBecause`;
- D11 (what `pipelineReady` means), which becomes moot;
- in the prose: "which artifacts are ready for the next stage" (contract introduction); "freshness and structural
  roots follow it" and "does not take part in freshness or structural roots" (R-P4's two relations, which keep
  "freshness"); "structural roots or `pipelineReady`" (R-E9's last sentence); and "the normalized schema is the
  structural source of truth" (README).

**Unchanged:** the layout, names, records and revisions; never overwriting; integrity; conflicts and their merge;
out-of-date tracking through declared inputs (R-E6); history (R-E9); work in progress; safe paths; and the stage
registry itself (the folders and their single writers are structure).

**Nothing is lost.** Every artifact in the report still carries `inStageCurrent`, `upToDate` with `staleBecause`,
and `historyVerified`. A tool that wants its own idea of "ready", for example "my input is current and up to date",
computes it from those and from its own record's inputs. The contract only stops prescribing which stage must feed
which.

**No project file changes shape.** `pipelineReady` appears only in the evaluation and its report. No
`edge-project.jsonld`, record or revision contains it.

## Decisions needed

### Q1. How to version it

| Option | What | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | One number for everything: contract **EPS v0.2**, `contractVersion: 2` in every file and in the report, context `eps-v2.jsonld`, schemas `*-v2` | One number with one meaning. Nothing outside this repository uses v1, so the only v1 folders are the generated fixtures and M1's demo folders | A folder written by M1's `eps` tool today would read as `E-UNSUPPORTED-VERSION` under v0.2 |
| B | Project files stay at `contractVersion: 1`; only the report and context get version 2 | No folder needs migrating | Two version numbers that mean different things, and every report-only change ever after raises the question again |

With A, I'd also propose an annotated tag **`eps-v0.1`** on `7526a69`, so the frozen text stays exactly
addressable after `spec/` moves on. Tags are immutable, which is what a frozen version needs. The v0.2 module reads
v2 only. Supporting both is not worth it with no v1 adopters.

### Q2. The examples

All 25 keep their numbers and folders; every expected report loses the two fields. The three that existed for R-E7
change purpose:

| Example | Now | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| EX-07 sdd-provisional | an SDD built straight from a source schema: up to date, but **not pipeline-ready** | the same folder, showing that the contract does **not judge an input's stage**: the SDD is up to date and current. Renamed **sdd-from-source** |
| EX-17 structural-root-mismatch | a diagram combining an SDD and a different normalized schema: STRUCTURAL-ROOT-MISMATCH | the same folder: two normalized schemas are just two ordinary inputs, and the diagram is up to date. Renamed **two-normalized-inputs** |
| EX-20 same-stage-dependency | staleness **and** the old structural root follow a same-stage dependency | keeps its point about staleness, which is unchanged |

The alternative for EX-17 is to retire it and leave its number unused. Since the folder still shows something true,
I'd keep it.

### Q3. Order of work

Recommended: implement v0.2 now as a short step before M2, then validate M1 once, on the v0.2 build. Only M1's
output lines change (`[pipeline-ready]` no longer appears in `eps status`); the two-PC check (M1 step 3) is
unaffected.

Estimated work: about half a day. It touches the contract text, context, schemas, the example generator (all 25
reports regenerated), `src/contract.mjs` (the structural-roots walk goes), `bin/eps.mjs`'s summary, about ten test
assertions, the M1 demo's expectations and the validation document. Then `npm run check` and a fresh review ZIP.

## Decision (2026-11-04)

Relayed from the registrar by SDD (Claude, an agent, at Aaron Damiano's direction):

- **Approved:** the removal as written; **Q1 option A**, including the annotated tag `eps-v0.1` on `7526a69`;
  **Q2**; **Q3**; items **2, 3, 4 and 6**.
- **Item 5, changed:** do not stop the evaluation on an unreadable file, because a browser tool such as SDD could
  then not open the whole project. Report the file as unreadable instead, with a new diagnostic naming the path and
  the error, and mark whatever depends on it as **not evaluable** rather than out of date or damaged. `eps` may
  still exit non-zero on it. Keep the offline step in the two-PC check.
- **Q2, clarified:** the folders of EX-07 and EX-17 are **renamed** (`EX-07-sdd-from-source`,
  `EX-17-two-normalized-inputs`), not only re-described. Their numbers are unchanged.

### How item 5 was implemented (details for the registrar to confirm)

The direction left the details open; these are the choices made, all in EPS R-E10, R-E4–R-E6, R-E9 and R-W4:

| # | Choice |
|---|---|
| U1 | The diagnostic is **E-UNREADABLE** with `path` and a new optional `error` field (the backend's code, for example `EIO`, `EACCES`, `UNKNOWN`, or a browser's `NotReadableError`). It is an `E-` code, so `eps` exits 1 |
| U2 | Only files the evaluation must read are reported: the project file, files named as artifacts, records or revisions, and files a record cites. A foreign, temporary or work file that cannot be read is classified by its name as before |
| U3 | "Not evaluable" is **`null`**: `upToDate`, `historyVerified` and `inStageCurrent` become true, false or null. Two new indefinite reasons, `INPUT-UNREADABLE` and `UPSTREAM-UNEVALUABLE`, and one history reason, `HISTORY-UNREADABLE`, say why |
| U4 | **A definite reason still decides.** An artifact that is out of date for another reason (for example a source file added) stays `false` even when one of its inputs cannot be read |
| U5 | A stage whose heads cannot be known (a revision file, its folder, or a current artifact cannot be read) gets the new status **`unevaluable`**, placed after `damaged` and `incomplete`, which are definite. Writers refuse to commit there, and refuse to cite the source collection while `source/` is unevaluable (R-W4) |
| U6 | A revision that cannot be read still names its identity in its file name, so it is never a missing parent |
| U7 | An artifact whose freshness is unknown (`null`) does not by itself make its stage `out-of-date`; its line in `eps status` says `not evaluable` |
